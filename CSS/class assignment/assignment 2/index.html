Mohmand District (Formerly Mohmand Agency)

Mohmand District, historically known as Mohmand Agency, is a culturally rich region located in the Peshawar Division of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan. Established as an agency in 1951, it operated under the administrative setup of the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) for decades. A massive historical shift occurred in May 2018 under the 25th Constitutional Amendment, when FATA was formally dissolved and merged into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, officially upgrading Mohmand from a tribal agency to a full-fledged district.

The district shares a highly strategic geography, bordering Afghanistan's Nangarhar and Kunar provinces to its west. Locally, it is surrounded by the Bajaur District to the north, Khyber District to the south, and the settled plains of Malakand, Charsadda, and Peshawar to the east. Its administrative headquarters is located at Ghalanai.

The terrain of Mohmand is primarily characterized by rugged, arid mountains and barren slopes. Because the landscape is dominated by steep hills and receives very scarce rainfall, natural running water is difficult to find outside of localized valleys like Gandab and Shilman.

Demographically, the district is home to over 550,000 residents who are predominantly from the Pashtun ethnic group and speak the Pashto language.

Culturally, society in Mohmand strictly revolves around Pashtunwali, the traditional unwritten ethical and behavioral code of the Pashtuns.

While traditional farming of wheat and maize occurs where soil permits, the economy of Mohmand relies heavily on its vast underground wealth.

Today, Mohmand District consists of seven distinct administrative tehsils. Following its integration into the mainstream provincial governance of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, the region has seen dedicated efforts toward modernizing infrastructure.
